# Registration research ideas

The [README](../README.md) lists the current shared 5k recipe. This notebook keeps observations worth following up, including gains confined to one case, motion range, or dataset. For each idea, explain what could produce the observation, how the proposed intervention acts on it, and which result would make us reconsider.

## Live mechanisms from the ten-hour session

**Optimize common anatomy and endpoint difference in separate Adam coordinates — tested, no shared gain.** The endpoint pair contains a common image component and a component that creates temporal change. Independent endpoint Adam mixes both gradients before estimating their second moments. If common reconstruction gradients dominate that estimate, useful difference updates may be suppressed; if the difference is mostly noisy, separating it could instead amplify that noise. Probe weighted gradients in `m=(source+target)/2`, `d=(target-source)/2` coordinates, then compare Adam in those coordinates against the same objective and endpoint-space clipping. The chain-rule gradients are `g_m=g_source+g_target`, `g_d=g_target-g_source`; after updates recover endpoints as `m-d`, `m+d`. Weight decay commutes with this linear transformation. A matched SGD control with half the transformed-coordinate LR checks algebra independently of Adam's coordinate dependence. Improvement without a corresponding increase in endpoint update magnitude would strengthen the preconditioning explanation.

Its large-motion hidden-feature correspondence margins and temporal-target correlations are lower. Better endpoint fits alone do not guarantee a useful parameter path. The independent-prefix control separates delayed registration from the common fit. A sharp single-endpoint prefix remains a possible test of blur versus identity locking; it should follow the control's result, with directional bias reported.

Initial common gradients were9–22times larger than difference gradients, but naive separation did not help registration. Delaying registration is harmful even without shared-image blur; improved reconstruction by itself is an insufficient criterion.

**Match hidden phase updates when changing width — tested local control.** A first Adam step atLR1e-4 moves hidden phases by roughly0.067rad atwidth256,0.10at384 and0.13at512, while the first-layer phase stays near0.0055rad. Width therefore changes the optimizer's functional step as well as capacity. Compare384 atits usualLR against1.3times hidden/headLR while preserving first-layerLR. Follow with256 at2times hidden/headLR if the first control supports this explanation. Charge recovery cost to the comparison. Matching the initial phase is a local control; it does not establish equal trajectories throughout training.


## Learn coarse displacement first, then refine local alignment

A vessel can be farther from its counterpart than the width of its local intensity profile. In that situation an image-similarity loss can have a sharp peak at the right alignment yet provide little directional signal at the starting position. Broad anatomical gradients could give the optimizer a direction while the mismatch is large. Once the vessel is close, its sharp boundaries could supply the precision needed to finish alignment. This explanation predicts different requirements for *finding* a correspondence and *refining* it.

The control held it at 0.50. Both used the recommended width-512 recipe and seed 42.


The intended curriculum is to establish displacement amplitude from broad anatomical structures early, then refine local alignment against fine detail. The pilot varied the spatial extent of the velocity grid while using full-resolution image evidence throughout. Small displacements can occur inside any crop. One possible explanation for their regression is that the schedule reached its finest grid only at the end of OneCycle, when the learning rate was already near its minimum.

Sampling also changed substantially. A 0.75-width box has about 27 times the volume of a 0.25-width box. With the same point count, the early box has much sparser samples per voxel, while the late box covers fewer anatomical regions per update. Measure points per physical volume and cumulative regional exposure alongside grid cell width.

For large motions, does a small step from the starting position toward the true match improve NCC more consistently after smoothing? Near the true match, does full-resolution NCC localize the peak more precisely? This evaluation-only probe isolates the image evidence from velocity-grid spacing and training dynamics. Smoothing can also erase a distinctive vessel. A flatter curve with no more reliable direction would weaken the rationale for blurred supervision.

**Let the probe determine the training test.** If smoothing extends the range of useful directions, apply it early to the images used by registration NCC, then restore full detail for refinement. A fixed crop isolates this change in image evidence. Compare checkpoint trajectories against an equal-length continuation at the previous scale, with the same learning-rate history. The final scores from the shrinking-crop pilot alone cannot distinguish these explanations.

**Can coarse and fine evidence cooperate within a step?** A broad image feature may supply a direction over a long displacement, while fine texture localizes a nearby match precisely. At a frozen field, compute registration-NCC gradients for a blurred and an original image on the *same* particles. If that separation appears, test retaining coarse guidance while introducing the fine term. Match the overall update scale and compare both motion bins and their signed errors. If both scales point the same way or both lose direction at large offsets, blending them has little mechanistic motivation.

## Does each update carry reproducible motion information?

**Gradient noise and objective conflict predict different failures.** Freeze an early, middle and late model. At each, repeat independent training draws and collect the weighted gradients of endpoint reconstruction (including moments), PD-LK and registration NCC on the same draw. For each term and layer, compare the norm of the mean gradient with the RMS deviation across draws. Measure pairwise gradient cosines and the norm of the summed gradient relative to the sum of individual norms. Repeat with the crop fixed and resample only particles or probe directions to locate the source of variability. Use disjoint draws to check whether a proposed step decreases its intended objective.

If the velocity probes dominate variance, spend the next compute on improving their spatial coverage at equal sample count. If repeatable reconstruction and registration gradients oppose each other early and align later, delaying registration has a specific rationale. An algebraic rearrangement with identical samples and exact gradients predicts identical updates in exact arithmetic. Any claimed benefit from rearrangement alone calls for a numerical parity check.

**When does anatomy make registration gradients informative?** Immediately after copying one SIREN into two, the interpolated image is constant in time and its temporal derivative is zero, even for a well-fitted shared image. The prediction concerns the first steps *after* the endpoints diverge. Compare a shared reconstruction prefix with two independently fitting endpoints over an equally long reconstruction-only prefix, as well as the existing joint start. Charge each prefix to elapsed time. This separates the benefit of giving anatomy time to emerge from the benefit of retaining a shared parameter representation.

## Which SIREN updates limit convergence?

- **Hidden-layer phase updates — one-step mechanism demonstrated.** A hidden unit responds to a change in the phase of its sine. Multiplying that phase by ω changes how far the function moves for a given weight update. With identical initial predictions and derivatives, hidden ω=15 with doubled hidden parameters matched ω=30 with half the hidden learning rate after one update. Repeat that matched comparison over 5k steps, recording phase changes on the same coordinates at each milestone. Try both a smaller and a larger hidden-layer update around the reference with the same initial function. Larger movement could accelerate a coherent update or disrupt useful spatial features. Use time to a common registration quality to decide which effect dominates and whether it changes during training. [Controlled probe](SIREN_CONVERGENCE.md#follow-up-hidden-omega-mechanism-probe)
- **Output initialization and motion along the parameter path — tested signal.** Endpoint supervision constrains two images. Recovery also depends on how intensities change between them as their parameters interpolate. Endpoint windows at true correspondences had nearly identical correlation. A discrepancy early in training would motivate testing the timing of registration supervision.
- **Capacity under a wall-clock budget.** Extra features could fit fine structure in fewer updates. A smaller network could instead benefit from more updates before the same deadline. Compare time to recovered registration quality across widths, including the dense recovery cost. [Performance investigation](PERFORMANCE_RESEARCH.md)
- **Learn a common image representation before splitting the endpoints — proposed.** Both SIRENs begin as copies of the same random function. A reconstruction prefix could give corresponding channels shared anatomical features before endpoint specialization. That might make parameter interpolation produce a more coherent temporal derivative. A single-image prefix could favour its direction, while drawing equally from both unregistered images could average structures across their displacement. At the split, measure sharpness and endpoint fidelity. Immediately afterwards, measure how quickly the temporal derivative aligns with the observed motion and how forward/reverse errors evolve. Compare to the direct paired start with pretraining and recovery charged to the same total time. If sharper features fail to improve the intermediate path, reconsider shared reconstruction as the limiting step.

## Which spatial scales transfer?

- **Input frequencies in physical units — proposed mechanism test.** First-layer weights moved only about 2–3% over 5k steps. The same initial ω therefore leaves a persistent spatial scale whose wavelength in millimetres depends on image extent. Measure first-layer phase changes over identical physical offsets across datasets, then compare a physically matched input initialization with uniform 30 on all three frozen sets. Keep native image sampling and velocity units fixed. [Frequency transfer and geometry](SIREN_CONVERGENCE.md#frequency-transfer-and-image-geometry)
- **Structure-centred crops — mixed pilot evidence.** A small boundary may supply a strong spatial gradient yet appear in few random crops. Increasing its exposure could help if infrequent supervision limits alignment. Global structure-weighted endpoint sampling improved OASIS Dice over uniform global sampling on all four pairs. A separate 50/50 mixture of ordinary and structure-centred shared crops reached 0.81819 Dice. Compare the direct-centering mixture against ordinary crops under the same shared 5k recipe. Measure small-label Dice and within-label patch NCC to see whether extra exposure improves alignment as well as reconstruction. Use image gradients for training and labels for evaluation. [Sampling methods and controls](STRUCTURE_SAMPLING.md)
- **Velocity grid spacing at fixed coverage — unresolved.** A single velocity cell pools image evidence from neighbouring locations. If their motions differ, a locally averaged velocity can underestimate motion even when the image contains a clear match. Earlier physical-grid trials changed several settings together. Vary grid resolution on the native images while holding crop coverage and physical smoothing bandwidth fixed.

## Route anatomical change through parameters that can express motion

**A coherent translation optimizer mode — tested, no broad gain.** If the first-layer frequencies and later layers are fixed, shifting the first-layer bias by the input matrix times a coordinate translation shifts the entire SIREN exactly. Linear endpoint bias interpolation then represents a finite translation exactly. Baseline first-layer phase updates are much smaller than hidden-matrix updates, despite this useful geometric route. Separate the joint first-bias gradient into a three-dimensional relative-translation component and its orthogonal residual. Optimize the coherent component with a1mm peak Adam step, retaining ordinary AdamW for all other directions and the same OneCycle multiplier. This changes optimizer geometry without adding a deformation predictor or changing checkpoint/recovery semantics. The exact-translation and gradient-projection identities pass synthetic checks;600 changing-crop captured updates pass.

**Paired sine/cosine input features — tested, no shared gain.** Translating a sine produces a sine/cosine combination. Explicit quadrature pairs let the first hidden matrix express the translation tangent while input frequencies remain fixed. The corresponding hidden-weight rotation formula matches the analytical translation derivative to about1.3e-7 relative error at initialization; the same formula on random unpaired features has order-one error. Training can break the initial quadrature relationship, and pairing halves the number of distinct input wavevectors, so this is a representation tradeoff rather than an automatic gain. Compare endpoint fidelity and transport alignment separately.

**Shared weight matrices with separate bias codes — tested, too restrictive for CT.** A reconstruction prefix gave the pair an identity correspondence before registration could influence it. A different possibility is to keep the anatomical matrices shared throughout joint training and let interpolated bias codes carry temporal change. This retains two endpoint SIRENs and the same analytic velocity/recovery. It removes independent endpoint matrix drift and makes the temporal derivative cheaper because all matrix directions are identically zero. Bias phases otherwise move far less per Adam step than hidden-matrix phases. Compare the ordinary bias learning rate with a30times sine-bias rate, corresponding to roughly0.09rad at the peak; keep output-bias updates ordinary to preserve the intensity-gauge control. Exact g,q,H,m and full shared-parameter VJPs match the general derivative chain. If fidelity falls while transport improves, test capacity before concluding that shared anatomy is unhelpful. If this helps, an independent-matrix bias-rate control is required to isolate the contribution of sharing.

For a path joining corresponding endpoints, the time integral of the brightness residual equals the endpoint intensity difference. The squared endpoint difference lower-bounds the integrated squared residual.

## What the later controls distinguish

The staged Gaussian fits transferred poorly to OASIS, with a folding increase on one pair.

The continuous Gaussian curriculum retained OASIS accuracy better than restarting the pair.

**More complete spatial observations can reduce noisy motion gradients.** At the same frozen model and crop, three rotated orthogonal pairs reduce field deviation and registration-gradient variation considerably more than two pairs. An independent three-pair control shows that the gain is partly geometric, beyond the extra two sites. Fixed coordinate-axis pairs have zero redraw variance but retain a larger field bias. Their reproducibility is therefore insufficient evidence of accuracy. Three pairs also raise actual training cost substantially. A rotated tetrahedron uses four sites, like the original two pairs, while giving an isotropic second moment in all three dimensions. Its frozen variance reduction is smaller but costs no extra derivative evaluations; a training comparison is warranted. Keep complete cell pooling and live gradients throughout.

OASIS slightly prefers uniform (0.82436 versus 0.82369). The proposal is improving something beyond simply reducing point count, but cached coarse predictions may locate fine residuals poorly. A future change should first measure correlation between cached and exact sampling scores at early and late checkpoints. Inverse-probability weighting preserves the full-crop population target; finite-sample normalized NCC ratios are not claimed to give unbiased gradients.

**Spend endpoint samples when the gradients need them — queued test.** The early 8k endpoint gradient still has a useful signal-to-noise ratio; late fine fitting is much noisier and benefits from 32k. A fixed 8k→16k→32k curriculum could save early matrix work while preserving the final 3k updates at full sample count. This differs from reducing the batch throughout training. The implementation consumes the same native 32k random streams and takes fixed prefixes, preserving crop choices, registration particles, probe directions and times. Optimizer state and the 5k schedule continue through the transitions. Test both accuracy and actual compilation/graph-transition overhead before treating the saved FLOPs as saved pipeline time.

The directional parameter derivative is `q = J_theta f · delta_theta`. Its spatial derivative is the mixed derivative `m` used in PD-LK.

**NCC-equivalent endpoint gauges can change registration — measured, post-hoc fix failed.** Equalizing the endpoints' global moments, or calibrating them to observed intensities, leaves endpoint Pearson NCC unchanged to about 1e-7 but worsens most tested registration scores. The temporal path contains information that endpoint NCC alone does not specify. This makes the coupled learned gauge part of registration, rather than an interchangeable presentation choice. Independent neuron permutations are another possible endpoint symmetry, but optimizing a discrete alignment is a low priority without evidence that the shared initialization lost its correspondence.

**Temporal recovery sampling is a useful speed lead.** The learned path may vary smoothly enough in time that 16 velocity evaluations are unnecessary. Holding the total spatial RK2 integration work at 64 steps, four evaluations give about 6 s recovery on 14 frozen cases with small score changes and converged PCG; eight evaluations are closer still. This tests temporal quadrature, separately from increasing spatial integration substeps on the same fields, which did not fix folding. Validate the final recipe and full cohort before selecting a lower fixed evaluation count. This is a fixed sampling choice within the retained PCG+RK2 recovery, not an adaptive solver.

The Gaussian cascade adds central folds at every stage. These observations do not support an image-edge particle-coverage fix as the leading explanation. A continuous velocity flow can strongly compress anatomy while the finite-difference Jacobian of its sampled map becomes negative. Compare the coordinate Jacobian of sparse integrated particles at folded grid sites against the official sampled-map Jacobian, then refine only the integration at those sites. Positive particle Jacobians with persistent negative map differences would support spatial underresolution/compression; negative particle Jacobians that disappear with finer steps would implicate the integration. Keep whole-grid folding visible and labels evaluation-only.

**Compilation changes the economics of adaptive point budgets — measured.** The 8k→16k→32k endpoint schedule preserves most quick4 quality, but three static point shapes add enough compilation and graph-transition work to increase whole-case time. Reduced endpoint FLOPs alone are not a useful optimization on short per-pair training. A future implementation would need one reusable shape or amortized compilation, and should demonstrate the payoff in actual dispatch-to-output time before more schedules are trained.

**Wider and shallower can be cheaper over the complete fit — in progress.** A 768-wide, three-sine-layer pair has about 50% more parameters than the 512-wide, four-layer reference, yet at 3k rather than 5k updates it uses about 10% less total dense forward arithmetic. It also composes fewer nonlinear layers along the interpolated parameter path. Compare against both the 5k reference and a 512/four-layer/3k control, using complete recovery cost and all three quick4 cohorts. An accuracy gain at equal update count distinguishes a useful representation change from merely compressing the schedule; actual wall time determines whether it is deployable. This experiment changes width and depth together, so it is not a pure capacity attribution.


Original-step particle positions match the dense map. This points to spatially underresolved/compressed maps in these examples, not a reason to discount the official folding metric. A fixed stronger PCG regularity control tests whether reducing compression preserves useful motion; a later representation study could test whether coarse-to-fine components create compression that survives their composition. Do not introduce evaluation-label gates or claim global flow positivity from sparse probes.

**Update budget and phase travel are different controls — in progress.** Compressing OneCycle from 5k to 3k at the same peak removes about 40% of the LR integral. The resulting brain loss can reflect insufficient optimization travel as well as fewer sampled observations. A 5/3 peak-LR control approximately matches the integral while keeping the same schedule fractions and decay, and should be judged by measured phase changes, instability/clipping, endpoint fidelity and registration. Adam preconditioning and stochastic gradients prevent an equivalence claim. Success would support a faster recipe; failure would direct attention to the number/quality of observations rather than simply increasing per-update movement.

A zero output head preserves the coarse path at initialization and initially blocks fine hidden-layer gradients. An exactly cancelling pair of opposite output heads can preserve that path while allowing the initial hidden gradients through.
